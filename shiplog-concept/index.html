01 / The gap

The warning signs rarely show up together.

A dashboard can tell you what moved. A support thread can explain what went wrong. An account note can tell you who matters. The hard part is connecting all three before the moment passes.

01 — SIGNAL↘︎

Usage starts to slide

One chart shows a dip, but it cannot tell you whether the customer is stuck, distracted or moving away.

02 — CONTEXT⌁

The reason lives elsewhere

The latest support ticket or a change in the account team may explain what the metric leaves out.

03 — TIMING↗︎

The next conversation matters

By the time someone joins the dots, the renewal call or expansion window may already be close.

02 / The approach

Make the next move easier to see.

Shiplog is the layer between customer data and the people responsible for acting on it: bringing signals into view, adding context and giving the team a sensible place to start.

A useful signal answers three things

What changed?
Why might it matter?
What now?

The goal isn't another wall of charts. It's a clear reason to look closer, with enough context for a person to make the call.

01

Bring the clues together

Read product activity alongside support, account and billing context instead of treating every tool as a separate story.

↗︎
02

Surface the change worth a look

See which account is behaving differently and what evidence might explain the shift.

↗︎
03

Give the owner a starting point

Turn the signal into a suggested follow-up. The person who knows the relationship stays in control.

↗︎
03 / The working rhythm

From scattered activity to a clearer call.

Customer intelligence is useful when it changes what a team does, not just what it knows.

STEP 01 / NOTICE

Something changed

A customer uses a core feature less, adds more users or raises a cluster of support issues.

STEP 02 / UNDERSTAND

Put it in context

Compare the signal with what else is happening across the account, not just the last data point.

STEP 03 / RESPOND

Make the next move

Give the account owner a grounded reason to check in, offer help or start an expansion conversation.

04 / A worked example

One quiet account can tell a bigger story.

Imagine an account where usage is down, the main contact has gone quiet and a renewal is approaching. Any one detail could be harmless. Together, they deserve a closer look.

Illustrative scenario only. Not live Shiplog data or a claim about current product functionality.
